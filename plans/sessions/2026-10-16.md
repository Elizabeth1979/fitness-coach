## Context / why
Elli's feedback during the session:
1. The old design was pastel, pink and "bleh", and the font was childish. She wants high
   contrast and a design that any child or person with a cognitive disability can follow.
2. The rainbow version that followed had too many colours, with no clear place to look and
   no clear next step.
3. There wasn't enough time to get from one exercise to the next, and "3-2-1" came out in a
   rushed burst.
4. She wanted a countdown at the end of each exercise, encouragement every time, and a gap
   to switch sides.
5. Her phone kept showing an old version after a deploy.

## Constraints that held
- The generator is pure and deterministic. Same inputs mean the same workout, and there's no
  `Math.random()` in the generator or engine.
- 10/20/30-min workouts stay within 45s of target (we tuned durations, not the tolerance).
- Tone rules apply to all spoken lines: never calories, weight, "burn" or guilt.
- Never interrupt a running workout. This now also covers app updates.

## Lessons for next time
- Check that a "deployed" claim really is the new build: confirm Vercel's deployment state,
  or search for text that only exists in the new code.
- Don't run `git checkout <old> -- src/` while there are uncommitted edits. It wiped work
  once today, and it was recovered from the stash.
- A git worktree needs `node_modules` linked before build, test or lint mean anything.
